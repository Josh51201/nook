# Nook — Requirements (living document)

_Last updated: 2026-10-08 (rev 34)_

## 1. Platform / Shell
- **CORE-001** Nook is a phone app containing many mini-apps ("Nooks") under one account.
- **CORE-002** Users add Nooks to their account; added Nooks appear on the homepage.
- **CORE-003** Nooks can be opened from the homepage and managed (arranged, removed, configured) easily there.
- **CORE-004** Heavy customization throughout ("to your heart's content").
- **CORE-005** Consistent cozy "small nook" naming theme for features and terms.
- **CORE-006** The Hearth (homepage) is fully customizable, with drag-and-drop arranging, so each person can set it up however they like (organized or otherwise). This is the app's headline appeal. _(Rebuilt as a free-form grid — see CORE-019.)_
- **CORE-007** Hearth tiles can be resized. _(Built: any size on the grid by dragging a corner, plus quick presets.)_
- **CORE-008** The Hearth can be restyled with colors and themes. _(First version built (proposed details): 7 tile colors, 4 Hearth themes (Hearth, Ember, Sage, Dusk), renaming the Hearth and each tile.)_
- **CORE-009** Nooks on the Hearth can be grouped into folders or groups. _(Now done with text labels placed anywhere on the grid (Title/Heading/Caption). Folders are still open.)_
- **CORE-010** Users can create widgets on the Hearth that show live info from a Nook (e.g. an upcoming Gathering). _(First version built: Wide/Large Gift Nook tiles show upcoming Gatherings, never anything about claims.)_
- **CORE-011** Users choose what they get notified about and what's shown on their Hearth. _(Gift Nook notifications are now chosen per person — GIFT-029. Hearth-level choices still later.)_
- **CORE-012** Each Nook has its own settings page for customizing that Nook.
- **CORE-013** The Hearth has a settings page for account-wide things: account info, setting your name, signing out, and anything else important. _(Now the Settings tab — CORE-025.)_
- **CORE-014** Every Nook has a clear way back to the Hearth.
- **CORE-016** Whatever you're typing in is never hidden behind the phone's keyboard, and every keyboard has a way to close it.
- **CORE-017** The app should feel alive and flow nicely — motion and animation, not static screens — and use a proper icon set instead of emoji. _(Built (proposed details): SF Symbols on iPhone / Material Symbols on Android via expo-symbols; press-in springs on everything tappable; lists that flow in and glide when they change; placeholder cards while loading; wiggling Hearth tiles in edit mode with smoother drag; iPhone large titles; respects the phone's Reduce Motion setting.)_
- **CORE-018** Users can add widgets of different kinds to the Hearth, each completely customizable in what it shows and how it looks — e.g. reminders to log food, countdowns to holidays and events, things to buy. _(Built (proposed details): Countdown, Coming up, Reminders (checklists with phone reminders once/daily/weekly — work in Expo Go), Gifts to buy, Sticky note, Inbox, Greeting, Card season (LET-009); each with title, Soft/Bold/Outline style, color, size, and its own settings, edited with a live preview. Ready-made ideas: Food log reminders, Holiday shopping list, Christmas countdown, Event reminders.)_
- **CORE-020** In Edit mode, tapping a Hearth widget opens its settings. Outside Edit mode it opens the widget to see what's in it (CORE-038); the Inbox widget opens the inbox, Gifts to buy opens your shopping list, and Card season opens the Letterbox's card list. Every Gift Nook screen opened straight from the Hearth has a way back to it.
- **CORE-021** Nook ships as its own app with its own icon (not inside Expo Go). _(Prepared: icon set drawn (a cozy fireplace in an arch, proposed), app.json and eas.json set up, build guide in docs/BUILDING_THE_APP.md. Needs Josh's Expo and Apple Developer accounts to build — see Q-025.)_
- **CORE-022** The Hearth gets built-out organization so it doesn't become one very long list as widgets pile up (a 4-column grid fills fast). Users can place houses or stumps on the Hearth; tapping one **enters** it — with a door-opening animation or similar — into its own user-organized screen. These are called **Hollows**. _(Built: tap a Hollow and its door swings open on its hinge, then you step inside as the screen grows into view. One level deep — a Hollow never holds another Hollow.)_
- **CORE-023** What's inside a house is as customizable as the Hearth itself: Nook tiles, widgets and text laid out however the person likes, with their own drag-and-drop design. _(Built: the inside of a Hollow is the same designable grid as the Hearth — Nooks, widgets, text; add, drag, resize.)_
- **CORE-024** Each house/stump can be customized — its name, its look (house, stump, …), its color — and putting things in or taking them out should be easy and friendly, e.g. dragging a tile or widget onto it. _(Built: name; look — a little house or a tree stump; color; size. Drag anything onto a Hollow on the Hearth and it lights up "Put in …"; let go and it's inside. Every tile and widget also has "Move to" (the Hearth or any Hollow). Removing a Hollow never deletes what's in it — everything moves back onto the Hearth. A ⚙︎ inside a Hollow edits the Hollow itself.)_
- **CORE-019** Users design their own Hearth layout — everyone's looks different. _(Built (proposed details): a 4-column grid where Nooks, widgets and text can go anywhere, gaps allowed; hold to drag (an outline shows where it'll land), drag a corner to resize, and anything in the way slides down. Older section-based Hearths convert automatically.)_
- **CORE-025** A bar of icons along the bottom of the screen, with a tab for creating and managing families (Burrows) — the same things the Gift Nook offered (start one, join with a Knock, invite, admins, leave, delete) — moved out of the Gift Nook so any Nook that shares with other people can use the same Burrows. _(Built (proposed details): three tabs — Hearth, Burrows, Settings; Nooks and Hollows open on top of the bar. Each Burrow has its own page with "In your Nooks" links, e.g. Gift Nook → its Gatherings and Wishbooks. The Gift Nook keeps each Burrow's Gatherings and links to the Burrows tab for starting, joining and inviting. Knock and hand-off codes are now entered in the Burrows tab. The Hearth's ⚙︎ moved to the Settings tab. Josh confirmed the three tabs — Q-027.)_
- **CORE-026** A short tutorial the first time someone enters: pop-ups with guides pointing at how things work, like adding things to the Hearth and creating Burrows. It shows **only** the very first time someone opens the app, or when they ask for it in Settings — never again on its own. _(Built (proposed details): a dimmed screen with a spotlight on the thing being explained and a bubble pointing at it, with Next/Skip. One tour: the inbox, Edit/drag (if anything's on the Hearth yet), Add, the Market, then over to the Burrows tab for starting one and entering a Knock, then Settings, ending back on the Hearth. It counts as seen the moment it starts, remembered on the account and on the phone. Settings → "Show me around again" replays it. If what it's pointing at is scrolled out of view, it scrolls there first.)_
- **CORE-027** An inbox in the top right of the Hearth shows when you have stuff to look at. _(Built: a bell with a little red count of unread notices; tapping it opens the Inbox. It's one inbox for every Nook: each notice says which Nook sent it and opens the right place, so new Nooks can send notices without changes here. A Nook's own bell (the Gift Nook's) shows only its notices. For now they all come from the Gift Nook — GIFT-029.)_
- **CORE-028** The Hearth gets more depth and pop, so it doesn't look flat. Josh left the how to Claude. _(Built (proposed details): tiles and widgets rest on soft layered shadows tinted to each Hearth theme (stronger on Dusk), with a thin lit edge along the top so they look raised; they sink a touch when pressed and lift higher while dragged; a warm firelight glow behind the top of the Hearth. Text and outline widgets stay flat.)_ Josh wants this style throughout the app. _(Built (proposed details): every card shares the same soft layered shadow; buttons and chips are raised with a lit top edge; text boxes sit slightly sunken; icon badges have a lit edge; every screen has a faint firelight glow behind its top; the tab bar floats on a soft upward shadow.)_
- **CORE-029** The Hearth's top bar always stays in view while scrolling, so the inbox and Edit are always reachable. The Hearth's title is more appealing than plain text. And while dragging with a full Hearth, holding an item near the top or bottom of the screen scrolls the page along with it, so it can be dropped anywhere (e.g. onto a Hollow) without letting go. _(Built (proposed details): the bar sits above the scrolling area and gains a soft shadow once you scroll; the title has a glowing flame badge and a small greeting above it ("Good evening, Josh"); Edit shows even on an empty Hearth; the page scrolls faster the closer your finger gets to the edge.)_
- **CORE-030** Widgets and tiles use their space well instead of leaving it blank — still simple, but more dynamic as they get bigger (like the Gift Nook's big tile with its white panel) — more things can be an icon, and there's more customization in general, so your Hearth feels like your home. Every Nook should show something on its tile when it's big (the Potluck tile was empty). _(Built (proposed details): each widget has its own layout per size — icon (1×1, an icon with a count), strip (one row), small, wide, tall and large — with inner panels, a tear-off calendar page for countdowns, a "Next up" spotlight for Coming up, a progress bar for reminders, a taped, ruled sticky note, and more. Every widget can shrink to a 1×1 icon. Nook tiles and widgets can each use a picked icon (48 to choose from), and Nook tiles get Soft / Bold / Outline looks like widgets. Small Nook tiles show a one-line "what's next"; the Potluck tile shows the next Potluck, what you're bringing, open spots and who's coming.)_ Josh: everything has to stay inside its box at any size. _(Built: Nook tiles list their info most-important-first and show only the lines that fit their size — a bigger tile just shows more; countdown pages, numbers and greetings shrink to fit; notes and lists stop at the last whole line.)_
- **CORE-031** Hollows are more exciting and fun. _(Built (proposed details): two new looks — a burrow hill with a round green door, and a spotted mushroom; idle life — chimney smoke on the house and hill, fireflies around the stump and mushroom; decorations — string lights that twinkle, snow (with falling flakes), or flowers; a burst of warm light when the door opens; and wide Hollows show little icons of what's inside. All motion calms down with Reduce Motion.)_
- **CORE-032** Your Hearth theme dresses the whole app — every Nook, Burrows, Settings, sheets and pop-ups — not just the Hearth, and the app supports the phone's dark mode. _(Built (proposed details): Hearth, Ember and Sage are light, Dusk is dark; "Go dark with my phone" (on by default) switches to Dusk whenever the phone is in dark mode. Picking a theme always shows it right away — if the phone's dark mode was overriding it, picking a light theme turns "Go dark with my phone" off, and Settings says when Dusk is showing because of the phone.)_
- **CORE-033** Each Nook's home greets you with some of the Hearth's personality instead of opening straight onto a list. _(Built (proposed details): a header card in the Nook's own color with its icon glowing, a big faint copy of the icon in the corner, a headline about what's next ("Christmas in 12 days") and a few quick facts (gifts to get, what you're bringing, who's coming). The Burrows tab has one too.)_
- **CORE-034** Opening a Nook from the Hearth feels like stepping into it, the way Hollows do. _(Built (proposed details): the tile grows to fill the screen, melting from its color into the Nook's background while its icon swells and fades, then the Nook fades in; coming back, it shrinks into the tile again. Skipped with Reduce Motion.)_
- **CORE-035** A cozier font, chosen by Josh after comparing options. _(Built for comparing: Settings → Font offers Classic (the phone's own, still the default), Rounded (Nunito) and Storybook (Fraunces headings over Nunito), each previewed in its own font; the whole app — screen titles included — switches instantly. Which one becomes the default is up to Josh.)_
- **CORE-036** Nooks are the special thing about the app, so the Nooks you add stand out from widgets on the Hearth. _(Built (proposed details): Nook tiles are little arched alcoves — a rounded arch top with a keystone, a carved inner frame, lantern light glowing down from the arch, a slow shimmer of light now and then, the icon centered in the alcove, and a deeper shadow than widgets. Widgets stay plain rounded cards. Opening a Nook grows it from its arch.)_
- **CORE-037** Every Nook is named "___ Nook" (Gift Nook, Potluck Nook, …). Each get-together inside the Potluck Nook is still "a Potluck".
- **CORE-038** Outside Edit mode, tapping a widget opens it up to show everything in it, in a useful, good-looking page — especially for widgets shrunk to an icon that show little on the Hearth. Edit mode still opens its settings. _(Built (proposed details): each page opens with a big card in the widget's own color and look. Countdown: a calendar page and the day count, a live days/hours/minutes/seconds ticker, and facts (what weekday it's on, sleeps to go, weeks and days, weekends before it), plus a link into the Gathering. Coming up: the next Gathering in a spotlight, then every upcoming one grouped by month, each opening its Gathering. Reminders and shopping lists: the whole list to tick off right there, a box to add items, hold to remove, "Uncheck all" and "Clear them" for checked-off items. Sticky note: the full note on a big taped, ruled page. Edit sits top right on every page. The Greeting widget still does nothing when tapped.)_
- **CORE-039** Widgets can be copied, so the same kind of widget can live in different Hollows or be styled differently. A Nook can be added more than once — on the Hearth and inside Hollows — and every copy opens the same Nook. _(Built (proposed details): in Edit, a Nook tile's or text's sheet has "Make a copy" — right here, the Hearth, or any Hollow; a widget's editor has the same, then opens the copy to customize. A copy is its own thing from then on (changing one doesn't change the other). A copied reminder list starts without phone alerts so nothing rings twice. The Market shows how many tiles of a Nook you have, offers "Add another", and its Remove takes every tile off (one at a time is from Edit). Hollows can't be copied.)_
- **CORE-015** Nooks are added to the Hearth from a shop-like catalog; the Gift Nook is one of the Nooks you add (nothing is on the Hearth until you add it). Removing a Nook only takes it off the Hearth; its data stays and comes back if you re-add it *(proposed)*.

## 1a. Accounts & Login
- **AUTH-001** Users sign in with their email address, via a one-time code sent to their inbox (no passwords). See Decision Log, Q-015.
- **AUTH-002** While developing in Expo Go, there is an extra email + password "Developer sign-in" so test accounts (made in the Supabase dashboard) can be used without waiting for an emailed code. It never ships: it does not exist in a real release build.

## 1b. Profile
- **PROF-001** Each person has a customizable profile: their name, a picture (a chosen icon or their initials on a chosen color), an optional birthday, and a short "about me". Family in their Burrows can see it; nobody else. They can edit it any time (Settings → Your profile). _(Built; the specific options — 16 icons, 8 colors, bio up to 160 characters, birthday shown as day only — are proposed.)_
- **PROF-002** A brand-new person is asked to set up their profile (name required, the rest optional) before they enter the Hearth, instead of showing their email as their name. New accounts start with no name rather than their email.
- **PROF-004** Each person can list their allergies and dietary needs on their profile; a kid's managers set the kid's. Family in their Burrows can see them, like the rest of a profile. (For Potluck warnings — POT-005.) _(The choices — peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, eggs, gluten, shellfish, fish, soy, sesame; vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, halal, kosher — are proposed.)_
- **PROF-003** A kid's managers can set up the kid's profile too — picture, birthday and "about me" — from Manage. Only their managers can (and their family sees it, like anyone's).
- **PROF-005** Each person can put their mailing address on their profile. It's optional but encouraged. Everyone in their Burrows can see it, except Burrows they choose to leave out (e.g. a work Burrow). It fills in family's Letterbox Nooks and stays current when they move (LET-004). _(Built: on your profile, with a chip per Burrow to leave any out. The Letterbox home nudges you to add it. Kept apart from the rest of the profile so it never reaches people outside the Burrows you chose.)_

## 2. Gift Nook (first Nook)
- **GIFT-001** Users create families and invite others to join. _(Now in the Burrows tab, shared by every Nook — CORE-025.)_
- **GIFT-002** Users add gifts they want to their own list.
- **GIFT-003** Gifts belong to categories; each category asks the right questions (e.g. Clothes → sizes).
- **GIFT-004** Gifts can include an Amazon link; direct Amazon integration if feasible.
- **GIFT-005** Family members can see each other's gift lists.
- **GIFT-006** Family members can claim a gift on someone else's list.
- **GIFT-007** The list owner can NEVER see claims on their own gifts. (A kid's managers aren't its owner, so they can see claims on the kid's list — GIFT-020. Once the kid takes the list over, GIFT-021, they're the owner and see nothing.)
- **GIFT-008** Everyone else can see what's been claimed on other people's lists.
- **GIFT-009** Gifts are either repeatable (many givers, e.g. money, clothes) or one-and-done (single claim).
- **GIFT-010** Supports many event types, not just Christmas.
- **GIFT-011** A person can belong to several families at once.
- **GIFT-012** A person's wish list is created at the account level, outside any family, and is visible in every family they belong to.
- **GIFT-013** Claims on a gift are shared across all families: if someone in one family claims it, members of every other family the owner belongs to see it as claimed too (the owner still never sees it, per GIFT-007).
- **GIFT-014** A family can have several events at once, e.g. Christmas, a specific person's birthday, an anniversary, a baby shower, or Other.
- **GIFT-015** Each event has an end date.
- **GIFT-016** After an event ends, it is archived and then removed after some period of time (see GIFT-027).
- **GIFT-017** Every event shows a person's whole wish list by default, but the person can include or exclude specific gifts per event (e.g. only money for their birthday, their full list for Christmas).
- **GIFT-018** Any member of a family can invite others by sending them a code (not just admins).
- **GIFT-019** Kids and people without phones get a managed profile: a parent creates it with just a name (no email). It has its own wish list and joins families like anyone else.
- **GIFT-020** A managed profile's creator is its manager and can add other managers (e.g. the other parent). Managers add and edit its gifts. Because the kid can't see anything, managers *can* see what's been claimed on the kid's list, to coordinate. Everyone else just views and claims.
- **GIFT-021** When a kid is older, a manager sends them a hand-off code. The kid signs up with their own email, enters the code, and the whole list moves onto their account. From then on they're the owner (GIFT-007 applies to them) and nobody manages it.
- **GIFT-022** Anyone in a family can suggest a gift on someone else's list. Suggestions are hidden from the list's owner; everyone else sees them and can claim them. A kid's managers see suggestions on the kid's list.
- **GIFT-023** A claim has a status: Planning → Bought. Other givers see it.
- **GIFT-024** Givers see who else is giving a gift and can leave notes ("got the blue one", "arriving 12/20"), across every family the owner is in. The owner never sees any of it.
- **GIFT-025** If the owner edits or removes a gift someone already claimed, the giver gets a heads-up (removed: "Mom removed this from their list" until they dismiss it; edited: "changed since you tucked it"). The owner is never told whether it was claimed.
- **GIFT-026** After an event ends, its honorees (or, with none, everyone; managers answer for kids) are asked "Did you receive any of these?" and check items off themselves. Received one-and-done gifts leave the list; repeatable ones stay. Nothing about who gave what is revealed. Only the owner checking a gift off removes it from their list. A claim made for that event keeps its status (e.g. still shows as Bought to other givers) for 30 days after the event, then is released if the owner didn't check that gift off.
- **GIFT-027** An event is archived at its end date and deleted 90 days later.
- **GIFT-028** Family admins can remove a member, rename or delete the family, make others admins, and delete any event. Other members can only delete events they created. A family always keeps at least one admin.
- **GIFT-029** Notifications, each turned on or off per person in Gift Nook settings: an event coming up, new gifts added, a gift you claimed changed, and someone joined your family. These start as an in-app inbox; phone push notifications come later (GIFT-031).
- **GIFT-031** Notifications are also sent as phone push notifications. (Later — not built yet.)
- **GIFT-032** *(proposed)* An owner can mark a gift ❤️ Most wanted; those show first on their list.
- **GIFT-033** *(proposed)* A gift can have an approximate price, and givers can filter someone's list by budget (under $25 / $50 / $100) and by Shelf.
- **GIFT-034** *(proposed)* Gatherings can be edited after they're planned (date, name, who it's for), with a calendar date picker and an "in 12 days" countdown.
- **GIFT-030** Pasting a link can fill in the gift's name and picture automatically where the shop allows it (Q-011).

- **GIFT-035** Each family automatically gets a Birthday Gathering for every member (kids included) who has set a birthday on their profile, created 30 days ahead, once a year, with that person as its honoree. It's created by whichever family phone opens Nook first, so nothing runs in the background. If an admin deletes one, it doesn't come back that year. _(Built; the 30-day lead time and "created by whoever opens Nook first" are proposed.)_
- **GIFT-036** Idea Stash: a person can keep private notes about other people — things they mentioned liking, gift ideas, sizes — that only they can ever see, and turn an idea into a gift (e.g. a suggestion on that person's list) when ready. _(Not built yet; details proposed.)_

## 2b. Potluck Nook
- **POT-001** A Potluck Nook for who's bringing what to a get-together, so nobody doubles up and nothing's missing. _(Built 2026-10-07 — in the Market, on each Burrow's page, and on each Gift Nook Gathering ("Plan a Potluck for this"). Proposed details: a spot can't take more dishes than the host asked for; signing up to bring something RSVPs you as Going; Potlucks are deleted 90 days after the day, like Gatherings; allergies also editable in Potluck settings.)_
- **POT-002** Every Potluck belongs to a Burrow, and that Burrow's members are who's invited. A Potluck can be tied to a Gathering (e.g. "Christmas at Grandma's" has both gift lists and a potluck) or stand on its own (a friends' or church potluck — make a Burrow for that group).
- **POT-003** The host can list what's needed ("2 sides, 1 dessert, drinks"), and people claim those slots; anyone can also just add a dish they're bringing.
- **POT-004** Dishes can be tagged with what's in them and which diets they suit.
- **POT-005** When a dish clashes with the allergy or diet of someone coming, the Potluck warns about it and names the person ("Contains peanuts — Mia has a peanut allergy").
- **POT-006** People RSVP — Going / Maybe / Can't make it — with how many they're bringing ("+2"), so the host knows how much food is needed. Warnings only count people who are coming. _(Proposed: a kid's managers RSVP for the kid; "Maybe" counts as coming for warnings.)_
- **POT-007** The day before, everyone bringing something gets an inbox reminder ("Tomorrow: you're bringing mashed potatoes"); it can be turned off in Potluck settings.
- **POT-008** Whoever makes a Potluck is its host and can add co-hosts; hosts and co-hosts can change it (slots, date, details). Burrow admins can step in too.

## 2c. Letterbox Nook
- **LET-001** A Letterbox Nook: the holiday card list with addresses — who you sent cards to and who sent you one, each year — that doubles as a contact list for family and friends. _(Built 2026-10-08 — in the Market. Proposed details: a home with card season and thank-yous at a glance, birthdays in the next six weeks, and every household (searchable once there are more than six); each household's page looks like an envelope with a stamp, with the people, their birthdays, notes and every card year by year.)_
- **LET-002** A Letterbox is shared by a household: you invite someone you already share a Burrow with, and once they accept it's one list for both of you. Either of you can mark a card sent. _(Built (proposed details): invites arrive in the inbox and in Letterbox settings. If your own Letterbox was only yours, its whole list comes along when you accept. Leaving gives you your own Letterbox again with a copy of the addresses; the card history stays with the household.)_
- **LET-003** Each entry in the Letterbox is a household — one mailing address, like "The Johnson Family" — with the people who live there listed inside. One card goes to each. _(Built (proposed details): the envelope name is suggested from the people — "Mia & Sam Johnson", "The Johnson family" — and can be changed. "Add family from your Burrows" picks several people at once, and people who share an address become one household.)_
- **LET-004** Addresses come from both places: family members' own profile addresses (PROF-005), which stay up to date, and addresses you type in for anyone not on the app (friends, neighbors, coworkers). _(Built: a household linked to family uses their profile address while they share it with you; otherwise the typed one.)_
- **LET-005** It tracks holiday cards each year: who you sent to and who sent you one, so next year you know who to send to. _(Built (proposed details): a Card season page per year — tick each household off as its card goes out, see who sent you one, and anyone who sent one but isn't on your list gets a nudge to add them. Cards that arrive in January count toward the year before.)_
- **LET-006** It tracks other cards too: birthday, thank-you, sympathy, wedding, graduation and more.
- **LET-007** You can keep photos of cards you receive (like family photo cards). _(Built: take one or pick one from your photos when logging a card; stored privately for your household only.)_
- **LET-008** This year's send list can be turned into printable mailing labels or a list to copy addresses from. _(Built (proposed details): labels for the households still to send, on standard 30-per-sheet address labels (Avery 5160 size), through the phone's print screen (which can also save a PDF); or copy all the addresses at once.)_
- **LET-009** A Hearth widget for card season: how many holiday cards are left to send, opening the list. _(Built: a "Card season" widget, any size down to an icon with a count, listing who's still waiting. The Letterbox Nook's own tile shows card season from October through January and the next birthday the rest of the year.)_
- **LET-010** A reminder about a week before a contact's birthday, so a card arrives on time. _(Built (proposed details): an inbox notice 7 days ahead, using the birthday on the person's Nook profile if they're linked; can be turned off in Letterbox settings.)_
- **LET-011** Thank-you notes owed: after you mark gifts received in the Gift Nook, the Letterbox lists who you still owe a thank-you note. _(GIFT-007/GIFT-026 mean the app can never say who gave a gift, so you say who each one was from yourself — it's never filled in from tucks. Built (proposed details): received gifts from the last year (yours and kids' you manage) wait under "Who from?"; then they're on a list to tick off as notes get written. "No note needed" clears one, and you can add thank-yous for anything else.)_

## 3. Open Questions
- **Q-003** For events about one person (a birthday, a baby shower), does the event show only that person's list, while group events like Christmas show everyone's? Who is the "honoree" of an anniversary (both people)?
  - _Built for now (proposed):_ honorees are optional when creating a Gathering. If any are picked, the Gathering links to their Wishbooks; if none, to everyone in the Burrow. Anniversaries can pick both people.
- **Q-019** What else should the Gift Nook's settings include?
  - _Built so far:_ "Hide gifts that are already taken" *(proposed)*, plus the four notification switches (GIFT-029). Saved to the account, so they follow you to a new phone.
- **Q-020** Deleting an account: Apple's App Store requires apps with sign-up to offer in-app account deletion. When someone deletes their account, what happens to Burrows they started, gifts they've tucked for others, and their Wishbook? (Needed before an App Store release, not before.)

- **Q-023** Emailed sign-in codes: Resend without a verified domain only delivers to its owner's own address, so family members can't receive codes. Which domain should Nook send email from (a domain must be bought, about $10–15/year, then verified in Resend and set as the sender in Supabase)? Needed before anyone besides Josh uses the app.

- **Q-024** Should each Burrow be able to turn automatic Birthday Gatherings off (so far only deleting a single one, which lasts for that year)? And should the 30-day lead time be adjustable?
